Calls

Missed-call note: after a missed call, the keeper pins a sticky note by the door with the caller's initial. Tapping it opens Recents.
Getting ready while it rings: in the second or two before you answer, the keeper tidies their hair or puts the book down.
Listening during a call: they nod when the caller's line appears, and lean in with a hand to their ear when Clarity is off and the audio is "muddy".
Hanging up: a short and happy call gets a wave. A long call leaves them stretching or yawning.
Mute: they put a finger to their lips, or zip their mouth.
Privacy mode: they draw a curtain across the window and turn their back to the phone number readout.

The caller's world

Local time: night callers ("11:05 PM their time") dim the room, the keeper yawns, and the lamp comes on. Morning callers bring bright light and a coffee mug.
What they're doing: "Driving" puts a steering wheel in the keeper's hands. "At the gym" gets a towel, and "At home" gets slippers.
Keepsakes: each regular caller leaves an object on the shelf, like a postcard from Nadia or a snow globe from Mama. The shelf grows the more you talk.
Their ringtone: the keeper dances a little differently to Chime versus Retro buzz.

Weather

Stronger outdoor effects: rain streaks down the window, snow slowly piles on the sill, and lightning briefly lights the keeper's shadow on the wall.
Changing weather: when the sky changes, the keeper swaps outfits in a quick spin rather than jumping straight to the new look.
Clear skies: they water a plant by the window, and it grows over the day.

Mood and time

A day-by-day mood: it builds from the day's calls, so the room gets messier when you haven't called anyone and tidier when you have. The existing "call someone soon" note already hints at this.
Bedtime: after midnight the keeper falls asleep in bed, not standing up.
Seasons and holidays: lanterns during Ramadan, and a cake on birthdays taken from a contact's details.

Things you can tap

Lamp: tap to switch it on or off. The keeper looks up.
Window: tap to open it; rain sounds get louder, and so does the keeper's shiver in a storm.
The keeper: tap to poke them. They giggle, and poking three times in a row gets a grumpy face.
Dragging: move the keeper to the bed to nap, or to the door to peek outside at the caller's weather.
Plant and shelf items: give each one a small reaction.

Theme and language

Case colour: switching it repaints the keeper's sweater and the room's wallpaper, not just the phone.
Arabic: the keeper's book shows Arabic script and the room layout mirrors. Their speech bubble could say "أهلاً".

What I'd do first:

Missed-call note and caller keepsakes. They make the room reflect your real call history.
Local-time lighting and the caller's activity props. They build on the weather system and data that already exist.
Tap on the lamp, window and keeper. It's cheap to add and makes the room feel alive.
